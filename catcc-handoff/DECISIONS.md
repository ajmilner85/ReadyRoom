# CATCC: decisions and findings

Running log of decisions made after HANDOFF.md. Where they disagree, this file wins.

## Hosting and environment (2026-11-07)

- **DCS server:** Fox3 Managed Solutions (`dcs.fightingstingrays.com`; Playground at `dcs2.`). We have file access to `Saved Games` (Scripts, Hooks, Mods), so we can install our own Lua hooks. Outbound connections from the host are probably restricted. Inbound mod ports are evidently allowed (Tacview 42674, LotATC 10310 and Olympus 3001 are all reachable).
- **Already on the server:** Tacview real-time export, LotATC, DCS Olympus v2.0.5 (web client on :3001), and DCS-gRPC (in `Scripts/DCS-gRPC`).
- **Credentials live outside the repo:** in `.env.local` / Fly secrets only.

## Seats

- **Typical:** 2 carriers × 2 controllers. The first controller is Departure + Marshal + Approach + Boss ("captain"); the second is the LSO.
- **Growth order:** a mission commander first, then per-boat approach/deck controllers. Up to 3 boats × 2.
- **Design limit:** 10 seats.
- **Model:** a seat is `(carrier, role)`, and one person may hold several roles. Command authority follows the roles:
  - the ship's controller steers;
  - the ship's controller or the mission commander launches tankers.
- **Supersedes the handoff:** this replaces HANDOFF §10's single global DEP/MARSHAL/APPROACH/FINAL row. Carriers are a list (up to 3), not "focus + other".

### Fuel

Tacview can't supply fuel. ACMI defines `FuelWeight`/`FuelVolume`, but DCS's exporter (DCS2ACMI 1.9.4) doesn't write them. The Week 5 cruise recording (1.3 M lines) has no fuel property at all, not even for the recording player's own jet.

A server-side Lua export **can** supply fuel. `Unit.getFuel()` in the mission scripting environment returns fuel as a fraction of internal capacity (above 1.0 with external tanks). Olympus exports exactly this, as an integer percentage. Converting it to pounds needs an internal-capacity table per type (F/A-18C: 10,860 lb, so 1 % ≈ 109 lb, which is enough for a `7.4` display).

**To verify live:** that `getFuel()` is accurate for *client* (player) aircraft on the server, not only for AI.

### Other facts found in the cruise recording

- `Coalition` is relative to the recorder (blue jets show as "Enemies"). Use `Color` for the side.
- Pilot strings are free text, e.g. `Hawk 4-2/733 QUIZNOS`. `parsePilot` extracts the callsign, a 3-digit board number and the handle. Roster matching should use the board number.
- `Group` matches the DCS flight, so formations come from the feed directly (HANDOFF §5.5).
- Several non-carrier ships report `AircraftCarrier` (e.g. `atconveyor`). Carriers are identified by type name (`CARRIER_HULLS`).

## Room service egress (Fly.io)

- **Ingress is free.** That covers the Tacview stream into the room service.
- **Worst case:** forwarding the raw stream to 10 clients is about 110 KB/s ≈ 0.4 GB/h, so ~1.2 GB for a 3-hour event, which is cents at Fly's per-GB egress rates.
- **Normalised deltas at 1 Hz with permessage-deflate:** roughly 10× less.
- **Conclusion:** telemetry won't affect the bill. Video would (see below).

## PLAT sharing (aspirational, documented per user request)

The capture tool (`Desktop/LSO-Diagnostic`) shares `LSOFRAME` as a D3D11 shared texture on the LSO's own machine. That machine must run DCS with the LSO platform selected. As of 2026-11-07 it has been tested in single player only, and it is pinned to a single DCS build.

1. **Other controllers monitoring the PLAT** (useful, but not on the critical path). The LSO station hardware-encodes the texture (NVENC/AMF/QSV via Media Foundation, zero-copy from D3D11) and publishes it to the room. Viewers receive it over WebRTC, which keeps latency under a second. Controllers then don't need DCS running to watch.
2. **A squadron-wide PLAT stream on a ReadyRoom page.** One publisher per boat pushes RTMP. **Don't relay it through Fly:** 1080p30 at about 4 Mbps is ~1.8 GB per viewer-hour. Push to an unlisted YouTube or Twitch stream, or to Cloudflare Stream, and embed that player in ReadyRoom. Expect 3–10 s of latency, which is fine for spectators.

## Own export hook (2026-11-08)

The user chose to build our own hook rather than reuse Olympus. It reuses LotATC's port 10310 (already open on Fox3; nobody uses LotATC), so the server admin doesn't need to open anything. Install steps are in `catcc-dcs/INSTALL.md`.

- **Design:** a GameGUI hook (`Scripts/Hooks/CATCC-hook.lua` loads `Scripts/CATCC/catcc.lua`) runs a non-blocking LuaSocket server and polls the mission environment once per second.
  - The poll runs `net.dostring_in('server', ...)` calling `CATCC_sample()`, which `sampler.lua` installs and reinstalls after a mission restart.
  - Protocol v1 is newline-delimited JSON. The client sends `AUTH <token>`; the token lives in `Config/CATCC.lua`, and the hook fails closed without it.
- **What it adds over Tacview:**
  - fuel (`getFuel()`);
  - true velocity (groundspeed, course and vertical speed come from DCS, not from position differences);
  - true-north heading, with the grid convergence correction;
  - unit/player/group identity, and the coalition as data;
  - deck, 2,000 ft and 10,000 ft winds plus sea-level temperature and pressure at each carrier;
  - mission date, local time and theatre.
- **Tacview stays configured** as a fallback source and for development.
- **Fuel in lb:** the internal capacities in `src/catcc/core/dcs/aircraft.ts` are taken from `M_fuel_max` in the local DCS install. For example, the Hornet is 4,900 kg.
- **Time:** DCS mission time is map-local. `THEATRE_UTC_OFFSET_H` converts it to UTC. The offsets are from memory, **so verify them per map** (especially Normandy/Channel and Sinai).
- **Testing:** `catcc-dcs/test` runs the real hook under fengari (Lua 5.3) with mocked DCS, LuaSocket and a mission environment on a grid rotated 3° from true north. DCS itself is Lua 5.1: the hook avoids 5.2+ features, and the harness shims `math.atan2`.
- **Not verified live yet:**
  - which `net.dostring_in` state reaches the scripting environment on Fox3 (`server` expected);
  - `getFuel()` for player aircraft;
  - CPU cost per sample with about 100 units.

- **Hosting:** it runs inside SDOBot for now, at `/catcc/ws` on the existing Fly app, off unless `CATCC_ENABLED=true`, because the user is watching the billing threshold. `catcc-server/room/main.ts` is the standalone server for when it moves to its own app.

It is verified with `/auth/v1/user` using the service key; the display name and board number come from `user_profiles` → `pilots`. Any signed-in user can view and claim seats for now. **TODO:** add a `manage_catcc`/`operate_catcc` permission before Phase 2 commands.
- **Frames:** a complete picture at 1 Hz, as compact `WireTrack`s, with permessage-deflate. Clients keep their own trails. That's about 11 KB per frame uncompressed for 43 tracks.

## Shared room state (2026-11-08)

- **What's shared:** radials plans (departure radials and stacks, per carrier hull) and marshal assignments now live in the room. They are validated by `cleanPlan`/`cleanAssignment` and broadcast to every subscriber.
- **Edit rights:** anyone signed in can edit until someone holds a seat on that carrier (or MC); from then on, only seat holders can. A seat in its disconnect grace still counts, so a reconnecting controller isn't locked out.
- **Cleanup:** an assignment is dropped when its aircraft leaves the picture.
- **Default stack:** until a carrier's plan is first edited, clients draw the CV-1 default stack locally. The first edit sends the full plan.
- **Persistence:** room state is in memory. It's lost on a room restart, and SDOBot deploys restart it. Persisting plans to Supabase per mission is backlog.

## Coordination line (replaces the handoff's ship-to-ship bisector)
- Automatic line: separator between each carrier's protected areas (ship + each active marshal hold, holds scored as whole areas). Margins 5 NM for ships, 2 NM for holds. If a clean band exists the line runs midway across the widest one; otherwise (holds of the two carriers overlap) it minimises the weighted depth it cuts into the margins, ships weighted 25x holds. Every bearing 0-179 deg tried; near-ties lean toward the ships' mean course. The original bisector ran through the other carrier's marshal stack, and the first max-margin version broke down when holds overlapped.
- Manual override per carrier pair, stored in the room plan as `coord[otherHull] = { brg, off }` (off = NM from our ship toward the other). Set numerically or placed on the scope (line perpendicular to the bearing from our carrier to the cursor, at the cursor range; one click sets; Esc/right-click cancels) from the Coordination section of the airspace panel; Auto clears it. If only the other carrier set one, it is reframed into ours.

## Realistic radar update rate (option, off by default)
- Display settings → Radar. Hits repaint only when the antenna sweep passes their bearing from the reference carrier: AN/SPN-43 (the CATCC marshal/approach search radar on Nimitz-class), 15 RPM, 4 s scan. Own ship always current; new contacts appear on first paint; lost contacts drop when the sweep passes their last position; history dots are past paints (one per scan).
- Driven by the wall clock (performance.now) so the painter and the sweep line agree. The sweep line is a Layers toggle (Reference → Radar sweep, off by default), independent of the update-rate option: one thin line at range-ring weight.
- Telemetry arrives at 1 Hz, so a paint shows the newest frame (up to 1 s old), not an interpolated position.

## Stack names carry the hull number
- Stack A on CVN-71 is "71A" (E and H likewise: 71E, 71H) on the scope, panel and assignment tags. Stored ids stay single letters. New-stack ids skip E and H (they were colliding with the helo stack).

## Marine layer (coastline + bathymetry)
- Layers → Marine: Coastline (land shaded, shoreline stroked) and Bathymetry (m). Shared, not per carrier.
- Water shading: each contour level fills the region shallower than it with a faint blue, so the water lightens toward the shore and the deepest water is the scope background. Water shallower than 15 m (a CVN draws ~12 m) gets an extra light-blue tint as not navigable.
- Contour lines shade from light (shallow) to dark (deep) blue; depth labels (metres) every ~30 NM along lines over 10 NM, kept upright, knocked out of the line.
- Data built offline by `scripts/catcc-marine.mjs <GSHHS_f_L1.shp> <cache-dir>` into `public/catcc/marine/<Theatre>.json` (~5.9 MB in all; only the current theatre loads). Sources: GSHHG 2.3.7 full-resolution shoreline (LGPL, Wessel & Smith) simplified to ~0.07 NM; NOAA ETOPO 2022 15" relief (public domain) via the CoastWatch ERDDAP at 30" (1' for the big open-ocean theatres). Contours by marching squares on the grid (and on a deep-padded copy for the closed fills), one Chaikin pass, Douglas-Peucker; short deep-water speckle dropped.
- Theatre from the source; if absent or the carrier is outside that theatre's box, inferred from position (`core/dcs/theatres.ts`). Nevada and Afghanistan have no file.
- Paths are projected about an origin near our carrier and re-projected only after it moves 10 NM.

## Tacview replay clock
- The replay fake hook used to report theatre "Normandy" (UTC+0) so its clock passed as UTC, which made every local time read as Zulu. It now infers the theatre from unit positions and sends local time like DCS does.

## Light chart
- Elevation axis is linear (one scale fitted to the day's extremes) so the sun path is the true curve; the old power-law mapping had infinite slope at the horizon and clamped the night.

## Marine layer fixes + Geography layer
- Contour lines are now the depth-fill rings minus their run along the data border (one geometry), so shading and lines coincide; the shoreline is cut from the simplified land ring itself (no stray stubs).
- Depth labels: open lines every 40 NM; closed loops by perimeter (one per 120 NM, at least one; none under 10 NM), evenly spaced.
- Layers → Geography: Place names (Natural Earth 10m populated places) and Airports / airbases (OurAirports: large and medium airports, plus military fields by name). Aerodrome symbol (ring + four ticks; military gold with a filled centre), ICAO id and short name. Decluttered by view width (major only beyond 250 NM, all within 60 NM). Both sources public domain; stored in the same per-theatre file (format v3, `--places`/`--airports` generator options).

## Map performance, unnavigable outline, territorial limit
- Measured: panning with bathymetry on ran ~25 fps (40 steps in 2.2 s) vs ~160 fps off; zoom similar. The depth layers (7 stacked translucent full-map fills, lines, hatch) were re-rasterised on every view change and every carrier update.
- The marine layer (depth, land, places, airports, territorial limit) now renders in its own memoised SVG in the layer's origin frame with a 35 % overscan margin, snapshotted with its scale-dependent props. Pans and carrier drift move it with a compositor transform; wheel zoom scales the snapshot and redraws ~250 ms after the last step (or beyond 2.5x). Re-snapshot also when the view leaves the margin. Pan and zoom now match the marine-off frame rate (~165 fps in the test).
- Land fill opaque (the hatch showed through at 0.92).
- Unnavigable (< 15 m) areas outlined in light blue as well as hatched.
- Geography → 12 NM territorial limit (off by default): dashed violet line 12 NM from the shoreline, from a ~0.5 NM land raster and an exact distance transform. Approximate: the legal limit is measured from baselines (closing bays, straight baselines along island chains), which no open dataset provides.
- LSO pattern view: viewBox now includes the upper turn (it started below the arc's top).
- Zoom: redraws after a zoom took ~100–130 ms each (rasterising the depth layer). ## Marshal entries (rework)
- Bugs found with a property sweep (`marshalSweep.test.ts`: 6 ranges × 12 bearings × 8 headings × 3 speeds × 3 hold sizes × 2 hands = 10,368 routes; every route must end at the fix, be continuous, bounded, go out on the correct side for its entry, and be established before the fix): 65 % of routes failed before the rework.
  - Transit: pure pursuit orbited a fix inside the turn circle, then flew straight for up to 1,000 NM (the long magenta lines). Now `direct()`: both turn senses simulated, shorter kept; a sense whose circle contains the fix flies straight until it doesn't; each try capped at distance + two orbits.
  - Join: the 3-try intercept gave up and jumped to the fix. Now `intercept()`: a capture law that holds a 45° cut to the lead point then flies an arc of 1.15 R onto the course (finite settling, no overshoot).
  - Teardrop and parallel angles: the textbook 30° / 45° assume standard-rate turns. With the CV 6-minute pattern (2-minute 180s, R = 2L/π) a 30° teardrop needs a 10+ NM leg to roll out on course. The offset is now θ = 2·atan(R / leg) (~35° standard rate, ~65° for the CV pattern), used for the teardrop leg and the parallel entry's intercept.
  - Parallel: the short turn to outbound could carry the aircraft onto the holding side; three ways onto the outbound leg (short turn, long turn, 30° cut) are flown and the shortest that stays on the non-holding side is kept.
  - Established at least 1.5 NM before the fix (0.5 NM after a parallel's intercept); otherwise the leg is lengthened, then a go-around out the inbound course (never needed in the sweep).
- Holding speed: aircraft slow to their max-endurance speed before the hold (`maxEnduranceKias`, approximate: Hornet 230, Tomcat 220, F-35C 250, Rafale 230, A-4 220, T-45 200, S-3 180, E-2 160, C-2 170, H-60 70, others 250). The entry is flown at that speed's TAS at the fix altitude with 1-minute legs and 2-minute 180s (`holdSpecForTas`), and the hooked aircraft's hold is drawn at that size. Fix time = transit at current speed with a 2 kt/s slow-down to holding speed (`marshalTimeS`) + entry at holding speed; the hook tag shows `→ HOLD 230 KIAS`.
- Cost: 0.27 ms per route on average (1.3 ms worst).

## Marshal entries: fly-by first turn
- Only the final inbound crossing of the fix is a fly-over. The entry's first turn at the fix is a fly-by (a fillet of the hold's radius begun R·tan(D/2) before the fix, rolling out on the line through it) when it is at most 150° and the entry's turn direction is the short way; otherwise the fix is flown over. Parallel entries go outbound on the radial (the first-turn sense is picked by side; the leg is lengthened if it does not establish).
- Direct and teardrop outbound headings are solved (θ near 0° / the 2·atan(R/L) offset) so the turn inbound rolls out on the course for the actual arrival, rejecting solutions with the leg on the wrong side; this removed the S-bend after the reversal (reported: a teardrop rolling out 1.2 NM short, then a 36° jog).
- Capture law is linear within 0.05 NM of the line (no chatter); a near-duplicate last point is replaced when snapping onto the fix.
- Sweep: correctness checks hold for all 10,368 routes; shape checks (outbound side, no S-bends > 10°) hold for every start ≥ 10 NM from the fix, with < 1 % slight misses closer in (29 routes, 2–7 NM starts, ≤ 1.2 NM to the wrong side or one 26° correction).

- **How DCS's character animation works (read from animator_logic.dll / animator.dll 2.9.31 with `dumpbin /disasm`, 2026-11-14; neither is packed, Flight.dll is):**
  - Each character has an `Animator::CharacterModelCookie` (pointer in a member at +0x70 of `AnimatedCharacterBase`; no exported getter; `set_override_animation(string)` begins `mov rcx,[rcx+70h]`). It derives from `model::ModelCookie` and `Animator::PropertyHolderImpl` (named float parameters) and holds a stack of `ShaderAnimation` records (0x44 bytes: id, +4 start, +8 duration, +0xC end, +0x10..0x18 loop, +0x1C/+0x20 blend ratio, +0x24/+0x28 transition, +0x30/+0x34 override).
  - `AnimatedCharacterBase::set_animation(int i)` = `cookie->set_animation(i, (float)Animator::get_model_time(), false, true)`. The third argument is the **loop flag**: the engine's own walking loop (`bs_move`) is applied with it on (transition word 0x0101), its one-shot walk start (`bs_move_start`) with it off (0x0100). That is why every signal we sent played once.
  - `set_animation(string name, string override)`: index by name (`get_animation_index` lower-cases its argument), played with the flag off, then the override. `set_idle(float, string)`: the idle the character falls back to (a generated timeline of idle variants). `AnimationController::change_animation_state(float, string, bool)` and the `AnimationStateMachine` exist (the animation graph: states, transitions with `loop`, `transition_duration`, `next_animation_mode`; the F/A-18 pilot's graph is readable at CoreMods/aircraft/FA-18C/animations/animations/typeV/graph/fa18c_anim_graph.lua, the deck crew's is inside `animations.edce`).
  - Every animation is an `Animator::Animation` ("blend space"): 330 plain ones plus 16 real blend spaces with named axes (`get_x_axis_name`, bounds, defaults), sampled at the value of the cookie's parameter of that name. `AnimationManager::get_animation_duration(name)` gives a plain animation's length.
  - The engine's debug inspector (`AnimatedCharacterBase::gui`) reads what a character plays with `cookie->get_animation(t)` → `manager->get_animation(record)` → `get_id()/get_name()`: the same calls give us a live readout.
  - The catapult crew's director does his come-ahead / left / right by replaying one-shot `Sign_TaxiFwd / _L / _R` from the behaviour tree (common.xml, `start_time_shift="-0.88333"`); the take-off crew's directors are driven from Flight.dll (packed), presumably through the `bs_sign_*` blend spaces: to be recorded, not guessed.
  - Also exported and not used yet: `CharacterMovable::net_send_start_move / net_send_start_stop / send_pos_sync / sync_pos / get_net_state / set_net_phantom` (the characters' own network sync), and constructors `CharacterMovable(viObjectNode*, const char* model, wPosition3<double> const&, float)` (a character made in code, no data list needed: the fallback if people cannot be added by data with the deck crew option off).
  - GroundCrew.lua has `static_crew = crews.static_crew`; crew.lua ships no such list; ModelViewer2.exe reads `static_crew.members` with the take-off crew's member loader. Catapult crews are `crews.crew[1..4]`, six members each, behaviour tree common.xml addressing members by name.

- **The approach radar: AN/SPN-46 ACLS tracks aircraft on final continuously (2026-11-22; asked: does the carrier have a second, faster radar for the approach / final, and if so should what it covers refresh faster).** Research verdict: yes, in substance. The SPN-43 (15 RPM, 4 s) is the marshal / approach-control search radar; the final approach has its own precision radar, the AN/SPN-46(V) Precision Approach Landing System (the ACLS radar; the AN/SPN-35 PAR on older ships, replaced by the SPN-46 on Nimitz-class by the mid 1990s). It is not a faster-turning search radar but a tracking radar: two Ka/X-band monopulse antennas, each locking one aircraft and following it continuously in range, azimuth and elevation to touchdown or waveoff, so two aircraft at once ("leapfrog": as one lands the next is acquired). Its data go to the CATCC operator consoles (radar display) and aircraft-control indicators, and as commands over Link 4A to the aircraft; the final controller compares the pilot's "say needles" with "the readout on the approach radar scope".
  - *Figures found:* range 8 NM (FAS) / 10 NM (Wikipedia; Navy approval in 1987 of "full automatic control from aircraft acquisition at ten nautical miles to touchdown"); azimuth 25 degrees, PRF 2,000 pps, precision 2 ft (Wikipedia infobox); antenna travel left 30 / right 60 degrees, +30 / -15 degrees ((V)3 FCC filing); lock-on "sometime between 8 DME and 4 DME" (CNATRA P-816). The SPN-42 (its predecessor) "locks on aircraft when the aircraft enters the acquisition gate ... then tracks the aircraft ... until touchdown or waveoff" (NAVEDTRA). No public source gives the SPN-46's track data rate in updates per second; a monopulse tracker measures every pulse, far faster than anything we can show. The SPN-41 (ICLS, "bullseye") is not a radar: two Ku-band transmitters (azimuth at the stern, elevation aft of the island) the aircraft receives one way; it sees nothing. The SPN-44 is a range-rate radar measuring approach speed for the LSO, not position.
  - *Sources:* FAS man.fas.org/dod-101/sys/ship/weaps/an-spn-46.htm, an-spn-41.htm, an-spn-44.htm, an-spn-35.htm; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/SPN-46 and /AN/SPN-35; electronicstechnician.tpub.com/14089/css/Significant-Interfaces-34.htm and An-Spn-46-V-Pals-33.htm (Electronics Technician vol. 4, radar systems); navyaviation.tpub.com/14030/css/Landing-Control-Central-System-An-Spn-42-206.htm; CNATRA P-816 (01-14) (murphysys.com/dcs/P-816.pdf), case III / ACLS lock-on; fcc.report/ELS/Sierra-Nevada-Corporation/0107-EX-PL-2014/145575.pdf (SPN-46(V)3 antenna limits).
  - *Modelled (`src/components/catcc/radar/spn46.ts`):* coverage 10 NM astern, 12.5 degrees either side of the final bearing (BRC less 9), elevation to the antenna's 30 degrees, airborne aircraft of our side. A free channel locks the nearest aircraft in the acquisition gate, inside 8 NM and 3 degrees of the centreline (the gate the Final view's LOCK-ON readout already uses); a lock holds while the aircraft stays in coverage and drops at touchdown, past the ship or out of the sector; two channels. The SPN-46 is a paint source of the focus carrier beside the SPN-43 sweep, looking at its locks only (`Radar.only`): a locked aircraft repaints on every painter step that brings a new frame. Everything else keeps the SPN-43's 4 s.
  - *Effective rate:* the data, not the radar, set it. The DCS hook samples at `rate_hz` (1 Hz in the shipped config), so a locked aircraft moves once a second (4 times the SPN-43 rate); raise `rate_hz` and it follows, up to the painter's 10 steps a second. The painter no longer repaints a track twice from one frame.
  - *Only while the ACLS is up* (Final view's Landing aids: ACLS UP / DOWN, `aids.acls`, up when unset as there), and *only on the Approach / Final display* (a CATCC seat's FINAL view): the SPN-46's data go to the final controller's displays, not the SPN-43 scope, so the RADAR view of the same seat and every other seat keep the 4 s picture for everyone. With "realistic radar updates" off nothing changes (the live picture at the data rate).
  - *Not modelled:* the lock being broken and re-acquired by the controller (bad "needles"), side-lobe locks, the beacon (APN-202 / 245) an aircraft needs for a Mode I / II lock (any aircraft in the gate locks), and the antennas' place on the ship (measured from the ship's reference point).

## Conflict alerts by relative motion, arrivals cut off at touchdown (2026-11-22)

- **The report:** Approach / Final showed many conflicts, e.g. a Case III sequence (FB 061, ship ~22 kt) with 712 at ~1.2 NM / 300 ft / 128 kt and 205 behind it at ~3-3.5 NM / 1,000 ft / ~150 kt, both red. That is a normal in-trail sequence: 712 traps in ~40 s, when 205 is still ~2 NM back.
- **Cause:** the red tags (and the radar's) come from `predictConflict` (`src/catcc/core/geometry/maneuver.ts`, via `findConflicts`). It projected every aircraft in a straight line for the whole 120 s look-ahead. So the leader on final flew on through the ship and the faster trailer "caught" it 1-1.5 min later, after the leader had already trapped. A pair inside the bubble that was opening or steady was a conflict too (CPA clamped to now). The separation bubbles were coloured by a second test, `sepViolated` in `finalModel.ts` (another aircraft inside the bubble now, no motion at all), so bubbles and tags could disagree.
- **Research.**
  - CV NATOPS lateral minima (positive radar control): 5 NM at 50 NM or more from the antenna, 3 NM inside 50, 2 NM on a designated approach or established downwind inside 12 NM, 1-1/2 NM established on final within 5 NM of the ship ([Navy Air Traffic Controller training manual, CATCC separation criteria, Table 11-6](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14342A/Table-11-6-Lateral-Separation-381.html)). These match `lateralStandardNm` and `sepStd`. Separation is lateral or vertical: a loss needs both lost (the same table's vertical counterpart, Table 11-7, and FAA JO 7110.65 ch. 5 §5, radar separation, which measures between target centres and applies the reduced in-trail minimum only on the same final approach course: [FAA JO 7110.65 ch. 5 §9 Radar Arrivals](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/atc_html/chap5_section_9.html)).
  - Approach control "establishes the interval for aircraft on final approach" and is responsible for separation until hand-off to final / PriFly ([same manual, Approach Control](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14342A/Approach-Control-408.html)). Case III pushes are a minute apart, which is the interval at the ramp; a leader still in the landing area when the trailer reaches the ramp is a foul-deck waveoff for the trailer (LSO / PriFly), not a radar loss of separation. No source found gives a radar rule for a pair whose leader lands first: the standard simply stops applying once the leader is on deck.
  - Pairs the standard applies to: every pair under control. The 1-1/2 NM figure is only for aircraft both established on final within 5 NM (in trail on the same course); crossing or opposite-direction traffic near the final takes the ordinary standard (here, in the carrier pattern, the existing 1 NM `PATTERN_LAT_NM`, see the pattern-traffic entry).
- **New rule (`predictConflict`):**
  - Each aircraft flies its course and groundspeed (climb / descent for 30 s, not below its marshal floor), sampled every 1 s plus the lateral CPA. The worst moment is where max(lateral / lateral std, vertical / 1,000 ft) is least; under 1 is a loss.
  - **Conflict** = a loss within the look-ahead that the pair is heading into: the worst moment is now or ahead. A pair inside the bubble that is opening or holding steady is not a conflict. A pass stays alerted for 6 s after its closest point (`CONFLICT_HOLD_S`) so it doesn't flicker at the abeam. A pair already inside and only creeping closer (under 5% of the standard: track noise) is not news.
  - **Landing cut-off:** an aircraft established on final (`onFinalOf`: astern of the ship within 12 NM, inside 6° of the centreline, course within 20° of the final bearing, closing on the ship at 40 kt or more, closure net of the ship's own course and speed) is predicted only up to its touchdown. A trailer that hasn't closed to the standard by then is clear. The ramp interval is shown by the slot timeline already.
  - **Standards:** both on final = in trail: 1-1/2 NM when the trailer is within 5 NM of the ship, else 2 NM (the old rule used the nearer aircraft's range and only inside 6 NM). Otherwise both in the pattern (inside 10 NM, below 2,500 ft): 1 NM. Otherwise `lateralStandardNm` by the nearer aircraft's range. Vertical 1,000 ft throughout (unchanged).
  - **Bolter / waveoff:** one climbing out ahead of the next arrival is opening and isn't a conflict. One on the downwind passing an arrival the other way is a conflict only if it will pass inside 1 NM with less than 1,000 ft (unchanged from the pattern-traffic entry).
- **Display:** the separation bubbles on the Elevation / Azimuth displays keep the standard's geometry (`sepStd`). They turn red when the aircraft is in one of the picture's conflicts (`sepViolated(a, pic.conflicts)`), so bubbles and tags always agree. The radar seats use the same `findConflicts`, so their alerts follow the same rule. No amber "compression" caution: the slot timeline already shows each arrival's time to the ball, and a new colour would be a new state the mockups don't have.
- **AZ/EL display: track table and primary track (2026-11-22).**
  - *Table:* top right of the scope, one row per aircraft the SPN-46 holds (the Landing aids LOCK-ON list, so two at most), nearest first: board number (else callsign), type as people write it (`typeName` in `scopeModel.ts`: F/A-18C, not the tag's F18C), ground range from the glidepath origin as the scope's tag gives it. Green dot on the primary row; the other row keeps the dot's space empty so the text lines up. Two rows and a flag line are always reserved and the table is absolutely placed, so nothing moves as tracks come and go. It is 136 px wide in the scope-label size (10.5 px mono) so it clears the HI and top "3" scale labels.
  - *Picking:* click a row, or a held track on the scope (24 px hit square), to make it primary. The primary is the bright contact with trail and tag; everything else in the field, held or not, is a ghost. The pick is this browser's (`localStorage` `catcc.final.azelPrimary`) and follows the aircraft id. When the picked aircraft, having been held, drops out of the SPN-46's holds, the pick is cleared and the nearest held track is primary again, so the next pass doesn't come back to an old pick. After a reload the stored pick waits until the room has that aircraft held again. A held track beyond the scope's 6 NM (the SPN-46 locks out to 8) is listed with its range but not drawn; if it is the primary, the scope has no bright contact until it comes inside.
  - *Primary not the nearest held track:* the primary row turns amber (`#E7B04F`, the app's attention colour) and an amber NOT NEAREST flag shows under the rows. The words say why the row is amber, and the reserved line keeps the table's size fixed. The other option the user named, an amber box around the row, would show the same state with no words. "Nearest" means the nearest of the held tracks.
  - *Nothing held (ACLS off in Landing aids, or nobody in the gate):* the table is empty and the scope works as before: the nearest aircraft in the field is the contact, the rest are ghosts.
  - Logic: `azElSelect` in `final/finalModel.ts` (tests beside it). Kit: the AZ/EL specimens pass the two demo arrivals as held, and the new `contact=not-nearest` variant picks the farther one (227 specimens, figma/catcc-kit regenerated).
  - *Seen from the ship (user call):* the AZ/EL is drawn as the controller sees it, looking out from the ship along the approach. Elevation is unchanged (high is up) and azimuth is mirrored: an aircraft high and right of course from the pilot's view shows high and left, and the controller reverses the direction mentally. `glideDeviation` keeps its sign (az + = pilot's right) and the mirroring is in the screen mapping (`azElX` / `azElY` in `finalModel.ts`, tested). ## Permissions computed by the database (2026-11-22)

- The user: "I do want the permissions re-work done so it can't be 'hacked' by the user." Closes the open item above: a signed-in user could write any `permissions` JSON into their own `user_permission_cache` row, and the RLS helpers and the frontend trust that row.
